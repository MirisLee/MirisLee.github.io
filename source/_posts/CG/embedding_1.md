In differential geometry, we know that any smooth manifold can be embedded in a Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$. In contrast, as any holomorphic function on a compact complex manifold is constant, we cannot put a compact complex manifold into any affine space $\mathbb{C}^n$. Therefore people turn to the embedding of compact complex manifolds in projective spaces, which will be dicussed in this post and the followings. 

Before considering the sufficient conditions for embedding, the necessary conditions are also essential. Recall that a submanifold of $\mathbb{P}^n$ is a K&#228;hler manifold with the hermitian metric induced from the Fubini-Study metric on $\mathbb{P}^n$. Another necessary condition comes from the study of line bundles on $\mathbb{P}^n$. 

## Hyperplane bundle on $\mathbb{P}^n$

Consider the line bundle $J$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$ given by $$J = \\\{(l, z) \in \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{C}^{n+1} \mid z \in l\\\},$$ together with the natural projection $\pi : J \to \mathbb{P}^n$. $J$ is called the **universal bundle** on $\mathbb{P}^n$. Using the coordinate covering of $\mathbb{P}^n$, we can give the trivializations of $\mathbb{P}^n$. Let $$U_k = \\\{[w^0 : \cdots : w^n] \mid w^k \neq 0\\\}, \quad k = 0, \cdots, n.$$ The local trivializations of $J$ are then given by $\varphi_k : \pi^{-1}(U_k) \to U_k \times \mathbb{C}$, $$\varphi_k(l, z^0, \cdots, z^n) = (l, z^k), \quad l \in U_k, \quad (z^0, \cdots, z^n) \in \mathbb{C}^{n+1}.$$ It follows that the transition functions of $J$ are $$\psi_{jk}([w^0 : \cdots : w^n]) = \frac{w^j}{w^k}, \quad [w^0 : \cdots : w^n] \in \mathbb{P}^n.$$

The **hyperplane bundle** $H$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$ is then defined to be the dual bundle of $J$, i.e., $H = J^\*$. In terms of transition functions, we see that $H$ corresponds to $\left(\\\{U_k\\\}, \\\{\psi'\_{jk}\\\}\right)$, where $$\psi'\_{jk}([w^0 : \cdots : w^n]) = \frac{w^k}{w^j}, \quad [w^0 : \cdots : w^n] \in \mathbb{P}^n.$$

In order to see why the line bundle $H$ is called the *hyperplane bundle*, we need to turn to an alternative definition of $H$. Fix a nonzero $(a_0, \cdots, a_n) \in \mathbb{C}^{n+1}$. The zero set $Z(F)$ of the homogeneous function $$F(w^0, \cdots, w^n) = a_0 w^0 + \cdots + a_n w^n$$ is a well-defined hyperplane on $\mathbb{P}^n$, and hence gives a divisor on $\mathbb{P}^n$. We claim that $H$ is exactly the line bundle $[Z(F)]$. Let $f_k : U_k \to \mathbb{C}$ by the holomorphic function given by $$f_k([w^0 : \cdots : w^n]) = a_0\frac{w^0}{w^k} + \cdots + a_n\frac{w^n}{w^k}, \quad [w^0 : \cdots : w^n] \in U_k.$$ It is direct to see that $$Z(F) \cap U_k = Z(f_k), \quad k = 0, \cdots, n.$$ Thus the divisor $Z(F)$ corresponds to $\left(\\\{U_k\\\}, \\\{f_k\\\}\right) \in H^0(\mathbb{P}^n; \mathcal{M}^\* / \mathcal{O}^\*)$. It follows that the line bundle $[Z(F)]$ given in terms of transition functions is $\left(\\\{U_k\\\}, \\\{g_{jk}\\\}\right)$, where $$g_{jk}([w^0 : \cdots : w^n]) = \frac{f_j}{f_k}([w^0 : \cdots : w^n]) = \frac{w^k}{w^j}, \quad [w^0 : \cdots : w^n] \in \mathbb{P}^n.$$ As $g_{jk}$ agrees with $\psi'\_{jk}$ for any $j$ and $k$, it is clear that $H = [Z(F)]$. 

---

Using the transition functions of $H$, a hermitian metric $h$ on $H$ may be express by a collection $\\\{h_k\\\}$ of locally defined positive smooth functions satisfying some properties. Suppose $\pi' : H \to \mathbb{P}^n$ is the natural projection and $\varphi'\_k : {\pi'}^{-1}(U_k) \to U_k \times \mathbb{C}$ is the local trivialization. Let $s_k : U_k \to H$ be the holomorphic section of $H$ on $U_k$ such that $$s_k(l) = {\varphi'\_k}^{-1}(l, 1), \quad l \in U_k.$$ It is clear that $h$ is determined by the positive smooth functions $$h_k(l) = h(s_k(l), s_k(l)), \quad l \in U_k,$$ and that these $h_k$ satisfy the following transition relations: $$h_k = |\psi'\_{jk}|^2 \cdot h_j, \quad j, k = 0, \cdots, n.$$ Conversely, given positive smooth functions $h_k$ on each $U_k$ satisfying the above condition, we obtain a hermitian metric $h$ on $H$ up to a constant scalar. 

In particular, we define $$h_k([w^0: \cdots : w^n]) = \frac{|w^k|^2}{|w^0|^2 + \cdots + |w^n|^2}, \quad [w^0 : \cdots : w^n] \in U_k.$$ It is direct to verify that $h_k$ satisfy the transition relations. The metric connection $D$ of $h$ is then given by the $(1,0)$-form $$\alpha = \partial h \cdot h^{-1} = \partial \log h,$$ and hence the curvature form is $$\Omega = \mathrm{d}\alpha - \alpha \wedge \alpha = \bar{\partial}\alpha + \partial \alpha - \alpha \wedge \alpha = -\partial\bar{\partial} \log h.$$ It follows that the Fubini-Study metric on $\mathbb{P}^n$ is expressed locally on each $U_k$ as $$\omega = \frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}\log\frac{|z^0|^2 + \cdots + |z^n|^2}{|z^k|^2} = \frac{i}{2\pi}\Omega.$$ 

## Positive line bundles

The above discussion about $H$ inspires the following definition of positive line bundles. Suppose $M$ is a complex manifold and $L$ is a holomorphic line bundle on $M$. Then we say that $L$ is a **positive line bundle** on $M$, if there is a hermitian metric $h$ on $L$ such that the associated curvature form $\Omega$ satisfies that the real $(1,1)$-form $$\frac{i}{2\pi}\Omega = -\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial} \log h$$ is positive definite everywhere on $M$. 

Note that if $L$ is a positive line bundle on $M$ with a curvature form $\Omega$ satisfying the above condition, then as $\omega = (i/2\pi)\Omega$ is a closed real $(1,1)$-form positive definite everywhere, it defines a K&#228;hler metric on $M$. Thus a complex manifold admitting a positive line bundle must be a K&#228;hler manifold. Moreover, if $M$ is a complex submanifold on $\mathbb{P}^n$, then it is not hard to see that $H|\_M$ is a positive line bundle on $M$. Therefore, *if a compact complex manifold $M$ can be embedded into $\mathbb{P}^n$, then $M$ admits a positive line bundle*. This gives a necessary condition for the embedding, and we will show in the following posts that this is actually a sufficient condition. 

---

The positivity of a line bundle can be described by its Chern class, as shown in the following propositions. 

**Lemma.** Suppose $M$ is a compact K&#228;hler manifold and $\alpha$ is a $(p,q)$-form on $M$. If $\alpha$ is $\mathrm{d}$-exact, then there exits a $(p-1,q-1)$-form on $M$ such that $\alpha = \partial\bar{\partial}\beta$. Moreover, if $p = q$ and $\alpha$ is real, then we may take $\beta$ such that $i\beta$ is real. 

> *Proof:* Since $M$ is a compact K&#228;hler manifold, the harmonic forms with respect to $\mathrm{d}, \partial$ and $\bar{\partial}$ are all the same. Let $G$ be the Green operator with respect to $\partial$, which is a real operator and also the Green operator with respect to $\bar{\partial}$. As $\alpha$ is $\mathrm{d}$-exact, it is orthogonal to the space $\mathcal{H}$ of harmonic forms, and $\partial\alpha = \bar{\partial}\alpha = 0$. Hence the Hodge decompostion of $\alpha$ with respect to $\partial$ is $$\alpha = \partial\gamma + \partial^\*\gamma'.$$ Note that $$\gamma' = \partial G \alpha = G\partial\alpha = 0,$$ we see that $\alpha = \partial\gamma$. Consider the Hodge decompostion of $\gamma$ with respect to $\bar{\partial}$: $$\gamma = \beta_0 + \bar{\partial}\beta + \bar{\partial}^\* \beta', \quad \beta_0 \in \mathcal{H}^{p-1,q}(M),$$ which implies that $$\alpha = \partial(\beta_0 + \bar{\partial}\beta + \bar{\partial}^\* \beta') = \partial\bar{\partial}\beta + \partial\bar{\partial}^\*\beta'.$$ It follows that $0 = \bar{\partial}\alpha = \bar{\partial}\partial\bar{\partial}^\*\beta'$. Recall that in the proof of the relation $\Delta_\mathrm{d} = 2\Delta_\partial = 2\Delta_\bar{\partial}$ we have shown that $\partial\bar{\partial}^\* + \bar{\partial}^\*\partial = 0$, implying that $$\\\|\partial\bar{\partial}^\*\beta'\\\|^2 = \\\|\bar{\partial}^\*\partial\beta'\\\|^2 = (\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^\*\partial\beta', \partial\beta') = (-\bar{\partial}\partial\bar{\partial}^\*\beta', \partial\beta') = 0.$$ Therefore $\alpha = \partial\bar{\partial}\beta$. 
>
> Note that the above construction of $\beta$ can be written explicitly as $$\beta = \bar{\partial}^\*G\gamma = \bar{\partial}^\*G(\partial^\*G\alpha) = \bar{\partial}^\*\partial^\*G^2\alpha.$$ In case $\alpha$ is real, we have $$\bar{\beta} = \partial^\*\bar{\partial}^\* \bar{G}^2 \bar{\alpha} = -\bar{\partial}^\*\partial^\* G^2 \alpha = -\beta,$$ showing that $i\beta$ is real. 

**Proposition.** Suppose $M$ is a compact K&#228;hler manifold and $L$ is a line bundle on $M$. If $\Omega$ is a closed $(1,1)$-form on $M$ such that $(i/2\pi)\Omega$ is real and $$\left[\frac{i}{2\pi}\Omega\right] = c_1(L) \in H^2(M; \mathbb{R}),$$ then there exists a metric on $L$ with the associated curvature form $\Omega$. 

> *Proof:* Take any metric $h_0$ on $L$ with the associated curvature form $\Omega_0$. Then by the definition of the Chern class, $$\left[\frac{i}{2\pi}\Omega_0\right] = c_1(L) = \left[\frac{i}{2\pi}\Omega\right].$$ Hence $i(\Omega - \Omega_0)$ is a real $\mathrm{d}$-exact $(1,1)$-form. By the above lemma, there exists a real smooth function $f$ on $M$ such that $\Omega = \partial\bar{\partial}f + \Omega_0$. Let $h = e^{-f}h_0$, then $h$ is also a positive function on $M$, and the curvature form oh $h$ is $$-\partial\bar{\partial} \log h = -\partial\bar{\partial}(-f + \log h_0) = \partial\bar{\partial}f + \Omega_0 = \Omega.$$

**Corollary.** Suppose $M$ is a compact K&#228;hler manifold and $L$ is a line bundle on $M$. Then $L$ is a positive line bundle if and only if the first Chern class $c_1(L) \in H^2(M; \mathbb{R})$ can be represented by a closed real $(1,1)$-form that is positive definite everywhere.
