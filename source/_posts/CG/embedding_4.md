In this post we finally state and prove the Kodaira embedding theorem that gives an equivalent condition for a compact complex manifold to be a submanifold of the projective space. 

Suppose $L$ is a line bundle on a compact complex manifold $M$. Then $H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L))$ is finite-dimensional, and hence admits a basis $\\\{s_0, \cdots, s_N\\\}$. We can consider the holomorphic map $$\varphi : M \to \mathbb{P}^N, \quad x \mapsto [s_0(x) : \cdots : s_N(x)], \quad x \in M.$$ The conditions for $\varphi$ to be an embedding are given as follows:
+ $\varphi$ is well-defined, i.e., there exists $0 \leq k \leq N$ such that $s_k(x) \neq 0$. This is equivalent to that $$\text{the restriction } H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L)) \to L_x \text{ is surjective.}$$
+ $\varphi$ is injective, i.e., $\varphi(x) \neq \varphi(y)$ whenever $x \neq y$. We claim that this is equivalent to that there exist sections $s, s' \in H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L))$ such that $s(y) = s'(x) = 0$ and $s(x) \neq 0, s'(y) \neq 0$, which happens if and only if $$\text{the restriction } H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L)) \to L_x \oplus L_y \text{ is surjective.}$$ Indeed, first suppose that $\varphi(x) \neq \varphi(y)$. With loss of generality, we may assume that $s_0(x) \neq 0$. If $s_0(y) = 0$, we just take $s = s_0$, and $s' = s_k - (s_k(x) / s_0(x))s_0$, where $s_k(y) \neq 0$. Otherwise there must exists $k$ such that $s_0(x)s_k(y) - s_k(x)s_0(y) \neq 0$, and then we take $$s = s_k - \frac{s_k(y)}{s_0(y)}s_0, \quad s' = s_k - \frac{s_k(x)}{s_0(x)}s_0.$$ Conversely, suppose $\varphi(x) = \varphi(y)$. Then there exists $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}^\*$ such that $s_k(x) = \lambda \cdot s_k(y)$ for all $k$. Thus such $s$ and $s'$ cannot exist. This proves our assertion for the equivalence. Note that the equivalent condition for $\varphi$ to be injective given above indeed implies the well-definedness of $\varphi$. 
+ $\varphi$ is an immersion, i.e., for each $x \in M$ the tangent map $$\varphi_\* : T^{1,0}\_x(M) \to T^{1,0}\_{\varphi(x)}\left(\mathbb{P}^N\right)$$ is injective. It is clear that this is equivalent to that the pullback $$\varphi^\* : T^{*1,0}\_{\varphi(x)}\left(\mathbb{P}^N\right) \to T^{*1,0}\_x(M)$$ is surjective. With loss of generality, suppose $s_0(x) \neq 0$. Then $\varphi(x) \in U_0 \subset \mathbb{P}^N$, where $U_0 = \\\{[z^0 : \cdots : z^N] \in \mathbb{P}^N \mid z^0 \neq 0\\\}$ is an open subset with coordinates $w^k = z^k / z^0, \\\, k = 1, \cdots, N$. Hence the surjectivity of the pullback can be expressed as that for any $\alpha \in T^{*1,0}_x(M)$, there exists $a_1, \cdots, a_N \in \mathbb{C}$ such that $$\alpha = \varphi^\*(a_1\mathrm{d}w^1 + \cdots + a_N\mathrm{d}w^N) = \mathrm{d}\left(\frac{a_1s_1 + \cdots + a_Ns_N}{s_0}\right).$$ If we let $$s = a_1s_1 + \cdots + a_Ns_N - \frac{a_1 \cdot s_1(x) + \cdots + a_N \cdot s_N(x)}{s_0(x)}s_0,$$ then $s(x) = 0$ and $\mathrm{d}(s/s_0)(x) = \alpha$. This may be expressed in a more intrinsic way. Denote the sheaf of holomorphic sections of $L$ vanishing at $x$ by $\mathcal{I}\_{M,x}(L)$. Consider the map $$\mathrm{d}\_x : H^0(M; \mathcal{I}\_{M,x}(L)) \to T^{*1,0}\_x(M) \otimes L_x, \quad s \mapsto \mathrm{d}(s/s')(x) \otimes s'(x),$$ where $s' \in H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L))$ satisfies that $s'(x) \neq 0$. This map is well-defined in view of the following computation: $$\mathrm{d}(s/s'')(x) = \mathrm{d}(s/s')(x) \cdot (s'/s'')(x) + (s/s')(x) \cdot \mathrm{d}(s'/s'')(x) = \mathrm{d}(s/s')(x) \cdot \frac{s'(x)}{s''(x)}.$$ It is not hard to see that $\varphi$ is an immersion if and only if $$\mathrm{d}\_x : H^0(M; \mathcal{I}\_{M,x}(L)) \to T^{*1,0}\_x(M) \otimes L_x \text{ is surjective.}$$ 

**Theorem (Kodaira embedding theorem).** Suppose $M$ is a compact complex manifold and $L$ is a positive line bundle on $M$. Then there exists $k_0 > 0$ such that for any $k \geq k_0$, the holomorphic map $\varphi : M \to \mathbb{P}^N$ induced by a basis of $H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L))$ is an embedding. 

> *Proof:* In view of the preceding discussions, we need the following two steps:
> (1) there exists $k_1 > 0$ such that for any $k \geq k_1$ and any distinct $x, y \in M$, the restriction $H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L^k)) \to L_x \oplus L_y$ is surjective;
> (2) there exists $k_2 > 0$ such that for any $k \geq k_2$ and any $x \in M$, the differential map $H^0(M; \mathcal{I}\_{M,x}(L)) \to T^{*1,0}_x(M) \otimes L_x$ is surjective. 
> 
> We may actually reduce the above assertions to the case when $x$ and $y$ are fixed. This is because if $\varphi(x) \neq \varphi(y)$, then $\varphi(x') \neq \varphi(y')$ for $x'$ and $y'$ near $x$ and $y$, and similarly for the condition that $\varphi_\*$ is injective at $x$. Hence we can take the same bounds $k_1$ and $k_2$ in a neighborhood, and then the compactness of $M$ implies that the global bounds can be taken. 
>
> **Step (1).** Suppose $\pi : \tilde{M} \to M$ is the blow-up of $M$ at $x$ and $y$, with the exceptional divisors $E_x$ and $E_y$. Let $E = E_x + E_y$. For each $k$, the pullback of $\pi$ gives $$\pi^\* : H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L^k)) \to H^0(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}(\pi^\*L^k)).$$ If $\dim M = 1$, then clearly $\tilde{M} = M$ and $\pi^\*$ is an isomorphism. Otherwise, for each section $\tilde{s} \in H^0(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}(\pi^\*L^k))$, by Hartogs' theorem its restriction to $\tilde{M} \setminus E = M \setminus \\\{x,y\\\}$ can be extended to $s \in H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L^k))$, and we can see that $\tilde{s} = \pi^\*s$. Thus $\pi^\*$ is again an isomorphism. 
> 
> Meanwhile, note that $\pi^\*L^k$ is trivial along $E_x$ and $E_y$, respectively, i.e., $$\pi^\*L^k|\_{E_x} \cong E_x \times {L^k}_x, \quad \pi^\*L^k|\_{E_y} \cong E_y \times {L^k}_y.$$ Since $E_x$ and $E_y$ are both compact, we see that $$H^0(E; \mathcal{O}\_E(\pi^\*L^k)) \xrightarrow{\sim} {L^k}\_x \oplus {L^k}\_y.$$ We can also see that the following diagram, where horizontal arrows are restrictions, commutes:

> Hence it suffices to show that the restiction from $H^0(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}(\pi^\*L^k))$ to $H^0(E; \mathcal{O}\_E(\pi^\*L^k))$ is surjective. Note that the direct image of $\mathcal{O}\_E(\pi^\*L^k)$ under the inclusion $E \subset M$ is a sheaf on $M$ (for which we use the same notation) with $H^0(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_E(\pi^\*L^k)) = H^0(E; \mathcal{O}\_E(\pi^\*L^k))$. 
> 
> Consider the following exact sequence of sheaves on $\tilde{M}$: $$0 \to \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}(\pi^\*L^k \otimes [-E]) \to \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}(\pi^\*L^k) \to \mathcal{O}\_E(\pi^\*L^k) \to 0.$$ Then we have the exact sequence $$H^0(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}(\pi^\*L^k)) \to H^0(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_E(\pi^\*L^k)) \to H^1(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}(\pi^\*L^k \otimes [-E])).$$ It remains to show that $H^1(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}(\pi^\*L^k \otimes [-E])) = 0$. Since $M$ is compact, there exists $k'\_1 > 0$ such that $L^k \otimes K_M^\*$ is positive for any $k \geq k'\_1$. Then the corresponding form of $\pi^\*L^k \otimes \pi^\*K_M^\*$ is positive semi-definite. Meanwhile, there exists $k'\_2$ such that $\pi^\*L^k \otimes [-E]$ is positive for $k \geq k'\_2$. Thus for $k \geq k'\_1 + nk'\_2$, we see that $$\pi^\*L^k \otimes [-E] \otimes K_\tilde{M}^\* = \pi^\*L^k \otimes [-E] \otimes (\pi^\*K_M \otimes [(n-1)E])^\* = (\pi^\*L^{k-nk'\_2} \otimes \pi^\*K_M^\*) \otimes (\pi^\*L^{k'\_2} \otimes [-E])^n$$ is positive. The Kodaira vanishing theorem then tells us $$H^1(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}(\pi^\*L^k \otimes [-E])) = H^1(\tilde{M}; \mathcal{O}\_\tilde{M}((\pi^\*L^k \otimes [-E] \otimes K_\tilde{M}^\*) \otimes K_\tilde{M})) = 0, \quad k \geq k'\_1 + nk'\_2.$$ 
>
> **Step (2).** Suppose $\pi' : \tilde{M}' \to M$ is the blow-up of $M$ at $x$, with the exceptional divisor $E'$. Similarly we have the isomorphism $$\pi'^\* : H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L^k)) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^0(\tilde{M}'; \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k)).$$ Moreover, note that $s \in H^0(M; \mathcal{O}\_M(L^k))$ vanishes at $x$ if and only if $\pi'^\*s \in H^0(\tilde{M}'; \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k))$ vanishes along $E'$, we obatin an isomorphism $$\pi'^\* : H^0(M; \mathcal{I}\_{M,x}(L^k)) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^0(\tilde{M}'; \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-E'])).$$ Since $\pi'^\*L^k$ is trivial along $E'$, we have $$H^0(E'; \mathcal{O}\_{E'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-E'])) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^0(E'; \mathcal{O}\_{E'}([-E'])) \otimes {L^k}\_x \xrightarrow{\sim} T^{*1,0}\_x(M) \otimes {L^k}\_x.$$ It is not hard to see that the following diagram commutes:

> It suffices to show that the restriction from $H^0(\tilde{M}'; \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-E']))$ to $H^0(E'; \mathcal{O}\_{E'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-E']))$ in surjective. Consider the exact sequence of sheaves: $$0 \to \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-2E']) \to \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-E']) \to \mathcal{O}\_{E'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-E']) \to 0,$$ which yields the exact sequence $$H^0(\tilde{M}'; \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-E'])) \to H^0(\tilde{M}'; \mathcal{O}\_{E'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-E'])) \to H^1(\tilde{M}'; \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-2E'])).$$ It remains to show that $H^1(\tilde{M}'; \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-2E'])) = 0$. There exists $k'\_3$ such that $\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-E']$ is positive for $k \geq k'\_3$. Thus for $k \geq k'\_1 + (n + 1)k'\_3$, we see that $$\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-2E'] \otimes K_{\tilde{M}'}^\* = (\pi'^\*L^{k-(n+1)k'\_3} \otimes \pi'^\*K_M^\*) \otimes (pi'^\*L^{k'\_3} \otimes [-E'])^{n+1}$$ is positive. By Kodaira vanishing theorem, $$H^1(\tilde{M}'; \mathcal{O}\_{\tilde{M}'}(\pi'^\*L^k \otimes [-2E'])) = 0, \quad k \geq k'\_1 + (n + 1)k'\_3.$$ 

**Corollary.** Suppose $M$ is a compact complex manifold. Then $M$ can be embedded into the projective space if and only if $M$ admits a positive line bundle.
